# African CITES Wood Species

Every CITES-listed tree taxon from the African region in the [[CITES Timber ID]] database (list as in force after CoP20, amendments in force 5 March 2026). Three lines per taxon: listing and range, wood and 10x end-grain features, and species included. Hand-lens identification only reaches genus or species group; confirm with an accredited laboratory (wood anatomy, DNA, DART-TOF mass spectrometry or NIR) and the live Species+ listing. Overview: [[African CITES Wood]].

- **Dalbergia spp.** (Rosewoods, palisanders, blackwoods, hongmu (part)): CITES Appendix II, #15 (#15: all parts and derivatives except leaves, flowers, pollen, fruits and seeds, finished products up to 10 kg of wood per shipment, and finished musical instruments). Covered: All species except D. nigra (App. I). D. cochinchinensis = #4; Mexican populations = #6. Listed since 2017. Range: Pantropical: Central & South America, Africa, Madagascar, South & Southeast Asia (c. 250-300 species).
- **Dalbergia spp.** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood very variable: purple-brown, red-orange, chocolate, nearly black; often streaked; sapwood pale, sharply demarcated; density ~750-1,300 kg/m3 (medium-heavy to very heavy); smell: many species fragrant (rose, spicy, sweet) when cut or sanded. diffuse-porous, medium pores, few; parenchyma aliform, confluent, banded; fine rays; ripple marks: yes; deposits: red-brown to black gum in some pores. Also: Rays and parenchyma storied: ripple marks often visible on tangential face; Fine, regularly spaced parenchyma bands (sometimes wavy) between pores; Rays very fine - barely visible even at 10x. Key diagnostic: GENUS LEVEL: diffuse-porous, few medium/large pores, fine banded + aliform parenchyma, storied rays (ripple marks), dark often fragrant heartwood. Treat any wood matching this pattern as SUSPECT Dalbergia (CITES App II) until proven otherwise. Look-alikes: Machaerium spp. (pau ferro, Bolivian rosewood - NOT listed); Pterocarpus spp.; Millettia laurentii (wenge); Swartzia spp.; Diospyros spp. (ebony); Bobgunnia; Microberlinia (zebrawood). Lab methods: DART-TOF mass spectrometry, NIR spectroscopy and DNA barcoding are used to reach species level; macroscopic = genus/group.
- **Dalbergia spp.** species and trade names included: Dalbergia melanoxylon (African blackwood, mpingo), East & southern Africa, Black-purple, extremely dense (~1,270), very fine texture; clarinets, oboes; Dalbergia baronii, D. maritima, D. louvelii and other Malagasy spp. (Bois de rose, Madagascar rosewood, palissandre), Madagascar, Red to purple; trade banned by Madagascar export ban + CITES zero-quota guidance. ~48 Malagasy species.

- **Guibourtia demeusei, G. pellegriniana, G. tessmannii** (Bubinga, kevazingo (rotary veneer), African rosewood, essingang): CITES Appendix II, #15 (#15: all parts and derivatives except leaves, flowers, pollen, fruits and seeds, finished products up to 10 kg of wood per shipment, and finished musical instruments). Covered: All populations of the three species. Listed since 2017. Range: Central Africa: Cameroon, Gabon, Equatorial Guinea, Rep. Congo, DR Congo.
- **Guibourtia demeusei, G. pellegriniana, G. tessmannii** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood pinkish-red to reddish-brown with darker purple or red veins; figured (pommele, waterfall); sapwood pale straw, distinct; density ~800-900 kg/m3; smell: unpleasant when wet/green, none when dry. diffuse-porous, medium pores, few; parenchyma vasicentric, aliform, confluent, marginal; fine rays; ripple marks: no; deposits: reddish-brown gum common in pores. Also: Gum (resin) streaks; occasional axial intercellular canals; Interlocked/ wavy grain. Key diagnostic: Pinkish-red, purple-veined heartwood; medium pores often gum-filled; aliform-confluent parenchyma; NO ripple marks (helps separate from Dalbergia and Pterocarpus). Three listed species cannot be separated from each other or from unlisted Guibourtia (e.g. G. ehie - ovangkol) by hand lens. Look-alikes: Guibourtia ehie (ovangkol/shedua - not listed); Guibourtia coleosperma (Rhodesian copalwood); Dalbergia spp.; Pterocarpus soyauxii. Lab methods: DNA/mass spec for species.
- **Guibourtia demeusei, G. pellegriniana, G. tessmannii** species and trade names included: Guibourtia demeusei (Bubinga), Cameroon to DR Congo; Guibourtia pellegriniana (Bubinga), Gabon, Equatorial Guinea, Cameroon; Guibourtia tessmannii (Bubinga, kevazingo), Cameroon, Gabon, Equatorial Guinea, Main source of kevazingo veneer and 'bubinga' slabs.

- **Pterocarpus erinaceus** (Kosso, African rosewood, vène, barwood, hongmu (part)): CITES Appendix II, no annotation: all parts and products are covered, including finished goods. Covered: All populations (also covered by the 2023 African Pterocarpus listing). Listed since 2016. Range: West African savanna: Senegal to Cameroon / Central African Republic.
- **Pterocarpus erinaceus** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood golden-red to purplish red-brown with darker streaks; sapwood pale, wide; density ~800-900 kg/m3; smell: faint, slightly sweet. semi-ring-porous, medium pores, moderate; parenchyma banded, aliform, confluent; fine rays; ripple marks: yes; deposits: dark red gum in some pores. Also: Distinct fine wavy parenchyma bands (Pterocarpus pattern); Pores tend to be larger in earlywood (semi-ring porous tendency); Ripple marks clear on tangential face. Key diagnostic: Red-purple 'rosewood' with semi-ring-porous tendency, fine wavy parenchyma bands and clear ripple marks. Top species in illegal West African hongmu log trade (round logs, squared beams). No annotation - all parts covered. Look-alikes: Pterocarpus tinctorius; Pterocarpus santalinus; Dalbergia spp.; Pterocarpus soyauxii (padauk). Lab methods: DART-TOF MS and NIR separate P. erinaceus from P. tinctorius/santalinus; DNA.

- **Pterocarpus tinctorius** (Mukula, African padauk (east/south), mulombe): CITES Appendix II, #6 (2019); African populations now also #17 (#6: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets and plywood; #17: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets, plywood and transformed wood (HS 44.09, such as decking and flooring strips)). Covered: All populations. Listed since 2019. Range: DR Congo (Katanga), Zambia, Tanzania, Malawi, Angola, Mozambique.
- **Pterocarpus tinctorius** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood bright red to deep red-brown, darkening with exposure; sapwood pale; density ~700-900 kg/m3; smell: none distinctive. diffuse-porous, medium pores, moderate; parenchyma banded, aliform, confluent; fine rays; ripple marks: yes; deposits: red gum. Also: Very similar to P. santalinus; often traded fraudulently as 'red sandalwood'. Key diagnostic: Red Pterocarpus pattern (banded parenchyma, ripple marks). Shipments of red logs out of southern/eastern Africa to Asia should be treated as suspect mukula/red sanders. Look-alikes: Pterocarpus santalinus; Pterocarpus angolensis; Pterocarpus erinaceus. Lab methods: DART-TOF MS / NIR.

- **Pterocarpus spp. (African populations)** (African padauk, muninga, mukwa, kiaat, barwood): CITES Appendix II, #17 (#17: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets, plywood and transformed wood (HS 44.09, such as decking and flooring strips)). Covered: Populations of Africa only. Listed since 2023. Range: Sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Pterocarpus spp. (African populations)** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood orange-red (padauk) to golden-brown (kiaat/muninga); sapwood pale; density ~550-850 kg/m3; smell: faint. diffuse-porous, large pores, few; parenchyma banded, aliform, confluent; fine rays; ripple marks: yes; deposits: red-brown gum. Also: Parenchyma in fine wavy bands alternating with pores; Storied structure. Key diagnostic: Same genus pattern as above. Proposal at CoP20 to remove Central African P. soyauxii was REJECTED - still listed. Look-alikes: Dalbergia spp.; Baphia nitida (camwood); Guibourtia spp.. Lab methods: DNA / mass spec for species.
- **Pterocarpus spp. (African populations)** species and trade names included: Pterocarpus soyauxii (African padauk), Central Africa, Bright orange-red; large pores visible unaided; Pterocarpus angolensis (Muninga, mukwa, kiaat), Southern & East Africa, Golden-brown, lighter; Pterocarpus osun (Camwood/ African padauk), West Africa; Pterocarpus mildbraedii, West & Central Africa; Pterocarpus erinaceus, P. tinctorius, See separate entries; Pterocarpus brenanii, Africa; Pterocarpus lucens, Africa; Pterocarpus rotundifolius, Africa; Pterocarpus santalinoides, Africa / Neotropics; Pterocarpus tessmannii, Central Africa.

- **Khaya spp. (African populations)** (African mahogany, acajou d'Afrique, khaya, lagos mahogany, mbawa): CITES Appendix II, #17 (#17: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets, plywood and transformed wood (HS 44.09, such as decking and flooring strips)). Covered: Populations of Africa. Listed since 2023. Range: Tropical Africa (West, Central, East) and Madagascar.
- **Khaya spp. (African populations)** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood pale pinkish-brown to reddish-brown; ribbon/stripe figure from interlocked grain; sapwood creamy to yellowish, not always sharply distinct; density ~500-800 kg/m3; smell: none. diffuse-porous, medium pores, few; parenchyma vasicentric, scanty; fine rays; ripple marks: no; deposits: dark red-brown gum in some pores. Also: Growth ring boundaries often indistinct (no or faint marginal parenchyma); Interlocked grain giving ribbon stripe on quartersawn faces; Woolly surface when sawn. Key diagnostic: Mahogany-type wood without ripple marks and usually with indistinct ring boundaries, interlocked grain. Hand lens: Khaya-group (vs Swietenia ripple marks + marginal lines). Look-alikes: Swietenia spp.; Entandrophragma spp. (sapele, sipo, tiama, kosipo - NOT listed); Lovoa trichilioides; Carapa procera. Lab methods: Microscopy/DNA for species; mass spec.
- **Khaya spp. (African populations)** species and trade names included: Khaya ivorensis (Lagos / Ivory Coast mahogany), West & Central Africa; Khaya grandifoliola (Large-leaf African mahogany), West to East Africa, Denser; Khaya anthotheca (White mahogany, mangona), West to East Africa; Khaya senegalensis (Dry-zone mahogany, caïlcédrat), Sahel / Sudanian savanna, Densest (~800); Khaya madagascariensis (Madagascar mahogany), Madagascar; Khaya nyasica (Red mahogany (East Africa)), East & southern Africa, Sometimes merged with K. anthotheca; Khaya agboensis (African mahogany), West Africa; Khaya euryphylla (African mahogany), Central Africa.

- **Afzelia spp. (African populations)** (Doussié, afzelia, pod mahogany, chanfuta, lingue, mkora): CITES Appendix II, #17 (#17: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets, plywood and transformed wood (HS 44.09, such as decking and flooring strips)). Covered: Populations of Africa only (Asian merbau Intsia and Asian Afzelia xylocarpa NOT covered). Listed since 2023. Range: Tropical Africa.
- **Afzelia spp. (African populations)** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood light brown to reddish-/orange-brown, darkening; sapwood pale, distinct; density ~700-850 kg/m3; smell: faint, slightly leathery. diffuse-porous, large pores, few; parenchyma aliform, confluent, marginal; fine rays; ripple marks: variable; deposits: YELLOW to whitish deposits in pores (characteristic). Also: Bright yellow/white powdery deposits in vessels (afzelin) - visible as yellow dots/streaks; Lozenge-aliform and confluent parenchyma, plus marginal bands. Key diagnostic: Large, few pores with YELLOWISH-WHITE deposits + lozenge/confluent parenchyma + orange-brown heartwood. Proposal at CoP20 to remove Central African A. bipindensis was rejected - still listed. Look-alikes: Intsia bijuga / I. palembanica (merbau - not listed, also has yellow deposits); Afzelia xylocarpa (Asian, not covered); Pericopsis elata; Milicia excelsa (iroko - has white deposits). Lab methods: Separation from Intsia needs microscopy/DNA - check stated origin.
- **Afzelia spp. (African populations)** species and trade names included: Afzelia africana (Doussié, lingue), West Africa savanna/forest; Afzelia bipindensis (Doussié (Cameroon)), Central Africa; Afzelia pachyloba (Doussié), Central Africa; Afzelia quanzensis (Pod mahogany, chanfuta, mkora), East & southern Africa; Afzelia bella, A. parviflora and other African spp.; Afzelia peturei, Central Africa.

- **Pericopsis elata** (Afrormosia, assamela, kokrodua, African teak): CITES Appendix II, #17 (#17: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets, plywood and transformed wood (HS 44.09, such as decking and flooring strips)). Covered: All populations. Listed since 1992. Range: Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Cameroon, Rep. Congo, DR Congo, CAR.
- **Pericopsis elata** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood yellowish-brown when fresh, darkening to golden/olive-brown (teak-like), lustrous; sapwood pale, distinct; density ~650-750 kg/m3; smell: none. diffuse-porous, medium pores, moderate; parenchyma aliform, confluent, vasicentric; fine rays; ripple marks: yes; deposits: occasional dark deposits. Also: Fine ripple marks; Interlocked grain; Unlike teak: not ring-porous, not greasy, no leathery smell. Key diagnostic: Teak-coloured wood that is diffuse porous with storied structure and aliform-confluent parenchyma (teak is semi-ring porous, oily, without ripple marks). Look-alikes: Tectona grandis (teak); Milicia excelsa (iroko); Afzelia spp.; Nauclea diderrichii (opepe). Lab methods: DNA/MS.

- **Diospyros spp. (Madagascar populations)** (Madagascar ebony, ébène, hazomainty): CITES Appendix II, #5 (#5: logs, sawn wood and veneer sheets only). Covered: Populations of Madagascar only. Listed since 2013. Range: Madagascar (c. 100+ endemic species).
- **Diospyros spp. (Madagascar populations)** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood jet black, sometimes with grey/brown streaks; sapwood pale greyish, distinct; density ~900-1,200 kg/m3; smell: none (slight acrid smell when worked). diffuse-porous, small pores, few; parenchyma diffuse, reticulate; fine rays; ripple marks: variable; deposits: black deposits in pores. Also: Very fine, close tangential lines of parenchyma (reticulate) hard to see in black wood - wet the surface or view sapwood; Pores small, radial multiples of 2-4; Rays very fine. Key diagnostic: Jet black, very heavy fine-textured wood; small pores in short radial multiples; fine reticulate parenchyma lines. Origin is decisive (only Madagascar listed). Look-alikes: Diospyros crassiflora (Gabon ebony - not listed); Diospyros celebica (macassar); Dalbergia melanoxylon; Dyed/ blackened woods. Lab methods: DNA/provenance; macroscopic ID = Diospyros genus only.
- **Diospyros spp. (Madagascar populations)** species and trade names included: Diospyros perrieri, D. aculeata, D. gracilipes and c. 80+ other Malagasy spp. (Ebony), Madagascar, Madagascar export ban + CITES zero export quota.

- **Osyris lanceolata** (East African sandalwood, African sandalwood): CITES Appendix II, #2 (#2: all parts and derivatives except seeds and pollen, and finished products packaged for retail). Covered: Burundi, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda, Tanzania. Listed since 2013. Range: East Africa (listed populations); species ranges into southern Africa, Mediterranean, Asia.
- **Osyris lanceolata** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood yellowish to light brown, oily; sapwood pale; density ~900 kg/m3; smell: sandalwood-like fragrance, weaker than santalum album. diffuse-porous, small pores, many; parenchyma diffuse; fine rays; ripple marks: no. Also: Very fine, uniform texture; pores barely visible at 10x; Often smuggled as roots and stem pieces for oil distillation. Key diagnostic: Small fragrant yellowish logs/roots from East Africa with very fine texture. Smell + origin are key. Look-alikes: Santalum album (Indian sandalwood - not CITES); Santalum spicatum. Lab methods: Oil chemistry, DNA.

- **Widdringtonia whytei** (Mulanje cedar): CITES Appendix II, no annotation: all parts and products are covered, including finished goods. Covered: All populations. Listed since 2019. Range: Mount Mulanje, Malawi (endemic).
- **Widdringtonia whytei** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood pale reddish- to pinkish-brown; sapwood pale; density ~450-500 kg/m3; smell: strong aromatic cedar scent. fine rays; ripple marks: no. Also: No resin canals; distinct growth rings; fine even texture. Key diagnostic: Aromatic reddish cypress-family softwood from Malawi; curios, planks. Origin key. Look-alikes: Widdringtonia nodiflora / W. cedarbergensis; Juniperus procera (African pencil cedar). Lab methods: DNA.

- **Prunus africana** (African cherry, pygeum, red stinkwood): CITES Appendix II, #4 (#4: all parts and derivatives except seeds, pollen and a few plant-specific items; for timber, logs, sawn wood, veneer, plywood and finished products are covered). Covered: All populations. Listed since 1995. Range: Afromontane forests of Africa and Madagascar.
- **Prunus africana** wood and 10x end grain: Heartwood pinkish-brown to reddish-brown; sapwood pale; density ~700-800 kg/m3; smell: bitter-almond smell when fresh (hence 'stinkwood'). diffuse-porous, small pores, many; parenchyma scanty; medium rays; ripple marks: no. Also: Mainly traded as BARK (medicinal - pygeum extract), not timber. Key diagnostic: Usually encountered as dried bark chips/powder (prostate medicine) - bitter almond odour. Wood is a fine cherry-like hardwood. Look-alikes: Other Prunus spp.. Lab methods: Chemistry of bark; DNA.
