# Precursor Chemicals

Quick reference on drug precursors and other chemicals used in illicit drug manufacture, built from the [[Precursor Chemical ID]] database (INCB Red List, 23rd edition, July 2025). Ask Jarvis things like "what is acetic anhydride used for", "what is CAS 108-24-7", "which chemicals are linked to fentanyl" or "what does the US control besides the INCB list".

**What is covered**
- 51 substances of INCB Table I (43) and Table II (8) of the 1988 UN Convention: [[Chemicals INCB Table I]], [[Chemicals INCB Table II]].
- 53 chemicals that are not scheduled internationally but are often found in clandestine labs: [[Chemicals Not Scheduled]].
- Which chemicals link to which drug (heroin, cocaine, methamphetamine, MDMA, fentanyl, LSD and others): [[Chemicals by Drug]].
- Chemicals that countries control nationally beyond the INCB tables, for 60+ countries and the EU: [[Chemicals by Country]].

**For each chemical:** name and synonyms, CAS number, UN number (only where one exists), HS code, control status, linked drugs, role, legitimate use and what it looks like.

**How to identify a chemical**
- By CAS number: the last digit is a check digit. An invalid CAS cannot exist, which is a red flag on a falsified label.
- By UN number: only dangerous goods have one. Many precursors have none, and a missing UN number is not suspicious by itself.
- By HS code: the customs tariff code, for example 2915.24 for acetic anhydride.
- By the company on the sticker: the tool checks whether the company is a known producer or supplier. "Not in the database" does not mean fake, and "known producer" does not mean genuine. Counterfeit and re-labelled containers exist.

**Limits**
- A reference aid, not legal advice and not a laboratory result. A label is never proof of contents. Confirm with a field test or lab analysis and keep the chain of custody.
- Salts, isomers and preparations of listed substances are also controlled, with different CAS numbers. A CAS that is not listed may still be controlled.
- Control status differs by country and region. In the EU, Regulations 273/2004 and 111/2005 apply; in the US, the Controlled Substances Act List I and II.
- The INCB's limited special surveillance list is for governments only and is not included.
